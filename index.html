🖥️ What Is Stardock Fences 5 Professional?

Stardock Fences 5 Professional is a desktop organization tool for Windows. If your desktop has slowly turned into a wall of shortcuts, files, folders, and icons you are afraid to delete because you might need them someday, Fences is designed for exactly that situation.

Instead of leaving icons scattered randomly across your screen, Fences lets you draw shaded boxes — called fences — around groups of icons. You decide what belongs together: work files in one shaded area, games in another, screenshots in a third. Each group gets its own labeled container that sits on your desktop and keeps everything in its place.

The Professional edition also adds rolling pages. That means you can create several full desktops worth of icons and flip between them, like pages in a notebook, without ever leaving your main desktop. You get the storage space of many desktops while looking at only one at a time.

🗂️ Organize Your Desktop in Minutes

After the first launch, here is the easiest way to get a tidy desktop without overthinking it.

Step 1 — Make your first fence

Right-click an empty spot on your desktop and choose the option to create a new fence. A shaded rectangle appears. Click its title bar and give it a name that means something to you, such as "Work" or "Photos".

Step 2 — Drag icons into it

Click and drag any desktop icon into the shaded area. The icon becomes part of that group. Keep dragging until the fence holds everything that belongs together.

Step 3 — Resize and place it

Drag the edges of the fence to resize it, then drag the title bar to move it wherever you like on screen. Windows will remember the position.

Step 4 — Repeat for other groups

Create a second fence for a different category and fill it the same way. Two or three groups are enough for most people, but you can make as many as you find useful.

Step 5 — Set up automatic rules

Open the Fences settings and look for the rules section. From there you can tell Fences where to file new screenshots, downloads, or documents automatically, so your desktop stays organized without any effort from you.

🔄 Rolling Pages and Shaded Groups Explained

These two features are the heart of Fences, so it is worth understanding what each one does and when to use it.

Shaded groups

A shaded group is a container. It has a background tint, a title, and an invisible border that keeps related icons together. Because the shading is translucent, you can still see your wallpaper behind it. You can change the color and transparency in settings if you want a lighter or stronger look.

Rolling pages

Rolling pages let you stack several sets of fences on top of each other and rotate between them. Think of it as a carousel: page one might be your everyday files, page two your project work, page three your reference material. You swipe or click to move to the next page, and the previous one rolls away out of sight. Nothing is deleted and nothing is lost — it is simply out of view.

This is useful if you have more icons than would ever fit on a single screen but still want them within easy reach rather than buried in folders. Combined with quick hide, you can keep a desktop that looks completely empty when you need it to, while still having everything one gesture away.

Tips for a Cleaner Desktop

  • Start with a small number of fences. Two or three groups are easier to maintain than ten.
  • Name your fences after what you use them for, not what is in them. "Current work" ages better than "January files".
  • Use rolling pages for projects that come and go. When a project ends, roll that page away instead of deleting anything.
  • Turn on automatic sorting for your screenshots folder. It is the single biggest source of desktop clutter for most people.
  • Try quick hide before a presentation or screen share — double-click the desktop and every icon disappears.
  • Keep one fence empty on purpose. It gives you an obvious place to drop new files before deciding where they belong.
